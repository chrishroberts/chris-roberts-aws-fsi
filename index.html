Point of View

In regulated buying centres, governance is the deal

Oxford Economics surveyed 200 Canadian business leaders for SAP and published on September 10, 2026. Read those numbers through a banking lens and they stop describing an adoption curve and start describing a procurement problem.

97%
Cannot govern it yet

Admit they are not fully prepared to deploy and govern agentic AI. Only 18 percent take a centralized, strategic approach.

73%
Data foundation is broken

Struggle with incomplete or inconsistent data, while 65 percent believe they are data-ready. That gap between belief and reality is where pilots stall.

48%
Agents acting wrongly

Report agents taking incorrect actions. In a bank, that is not a bug backlog. It is a regulator conversation.

What that means for a Canadian FSI territory

The bottleneck is not model capability and has not been for some time. It is the data foundation underneath and the governance spine running across it. With Anthropic and now OpenAI Frontier both on Bedrock, AWS has effectively conceded in its own architecture that the model layer is interchangeable. That is the right call, and it moves the commercial fight to exactly the layers where banks, insurers and payments firms are stuck.

Residency as a closing argument

Knowing where the data and the models actually run is the deciding question for a Canadian regulated buyer, not a feature question. Canada West gives that answer a physical address, and it belongs early in the conversation rather than in a security annex.

Reading the incumbent stack

The big five banks are deep Salesforce, Marketing Cloud and Data 360 environments. I sold that stack into this market for a decade, so I can read a Canadian bank's existing data and engagement architecture from the inside and position against it accurately rather than generically.

Audit-first sequencing

Demos sell, audits ship. In regulated accounts I have learned to open with privacy, governance and review rather than defer them, because deferring simply relocates the deal risk to the quarter you were counting on.

Building the team around it

A team selling into FSI needs a repeatable governance motion, not just product fluency. That is an enablement and operating-cadence problem, and it is the part of sales leadership I am best at.
